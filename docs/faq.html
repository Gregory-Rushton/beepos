Honey FAQ

What does “raw, unfiltered” mean?

Raw honey hasn't been heated or pasteurized, which large producers often do to keep honey liquid on store shelves. Unfiltered means it hasn't been forced through fine filters, so it keeps the small amounts of pollen that come with it from the hive.

My honey turned cloudy or solid. Is it still good?

Yes. That's crystallization, and it's natural. Honey is mostly two sugars, glucose and fructose, and over time the glucose forms crystals. Raw honey crystallizes sooner than processed honey, and cool temperatures speed it up.

Crystallized honey is good to eat and spreads nicely on toast. To make it liquid again, set the jar in a pan of warm (not boiling) water and stir now and then until the crystals dissolve. Avoid the microwave and don't let it get hot, since heat changes the flavor.

How should I store honey?

Keep it tightly closed at room temperature. The refrigerator makes it crystallize faster. Sealed honey keeps for years; keeping the lid on stops it from absorbing moisture, which can make it ferment.

Can babies have honey?

No. Don't give honey of any kind to children under 1 year old. Honey can contain spores of the bacteria that cause infant botulism, a rare but serious illness. Older children and adults aren't at risk in the same way.

Does local honey help with seasonal allergies?

Many people hope so, but studies haven't shown a consistent benefit. Most seasonal allergies come from wind-blown pollen from trees, grasses and weeds, while bees mostly collect the heavier pollen of flowers. Enjoy local honey because it tastes good, and talk to your doctor about allergies.

Why do your spring and summer honeys taste different?

The flavor and color of honey come from the flowers the bees visited. We harvest twice a year, so each harvest reflects what was blooming that season. Read more about our bees.
